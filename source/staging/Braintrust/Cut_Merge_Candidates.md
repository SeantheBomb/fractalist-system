# Cut and Merge Candidates

Based on the panel scores, the retrospectives, and a text check of the v3 draft (112.4k words; the source manuscript is 93.7k). This is analysis only: nothing here has been applied. Paragraph ids (P####) are from the current v3 draft (`Fractalism_Draft_v3_Fixed.md`).

## Why cutting is the lever, not more rewriting

The chapter rubric's second criterion, **Every Paragraph Earns** ("removing or relocating any one would leave a noticeable gap"), is where chapters fail. Across all 91 chapters its yes-rate in the latest scoring averages **49%**, against 95% for One Stated Aim, 61% for Order Builds to Arrival and 93% for Delivers Something Usable. In 36 chapters the aim and the usable step both clear (80%+) while this criterion sits at 40% or below, so it is the clearest drag (some are also weak on order); of the 18 chapters still below 3, the average earn-rate is 7%. Two rounds of rewording never moved this criterion because the only fix for "this paragraph could go" is to remove or fold it. That is what the reviewers meant by "reworked, never cut protects exactly the material I would cut."

The redundancy is **conceptual, not copy-paste**: a scan for repeated text found almost no verbatim duplication (the one exact duplicate was a review artifact, already removed). The weight is in chapters that re-make an earlier point, tables that re-tabulate another chapter's catalogue, and stacked examples.

## Do not cut

- **Safety and off-ramp material**: the grief acknowledgement and exits in Part Five, the Part Seven calendar off-ramp, the Weaving stop rule, the Loop Tongue blame guard and stop rule, and "not care, not treatment" in Part One. Reviewers (Clinician, Structural Critic, Stress-Tester) rated these as what the book does best; several cuts above are adjacent to them.
- **The losing conditions and the Ines paragraph** (Front Matter, Part Four): the World score rose from 3 to 4 partly because these exist.
- **Paragraphs holding the 95 Derivation Map anchors** unless the anchor is moved: 113 paragraphs. Every candidate above flags the ones it touches.
- **The 53 Standing Thirds** (your note that they are "still cooking"): the Part Five figures were not touched by any recommendation here except as the source that makes the Deck redundant.

## Expected effect

- **Words:** Tier 1 about 4,674; Tier 2 about 4,878; Tier 3 about 3,857 if each is folded or dropped by half. Together at most roughly 13,409 words, taking the book from 112.4k to about 100k, which is +7% over the source instead of +20%. The tiers overlap and several items are judgment calls, so treat the total as an upper bound; realistically expect about 60% of it (roughly 8,000 words).
- **Scores:** a prediction, not a measurement. Removing redundant paragraphs is the only thing that can raise "Every Paragraph Earns", and 36 chapters clear aim and usability but fail on this one. For the chapters that are also weak on order (Systems Theory, The Fold, Naming and Defusing, Reading Your Own Cycle), cutting is necessary but not sufficient; they need a reorder as well. The panel would need to rescore to confirm.
