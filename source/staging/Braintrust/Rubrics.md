# Agreed Review Rubrics by Scale

Each persona proposed four yes/no criteria per scale; one agent merged them; all ten ratified or amended; one agent finalized. Totals run 0 to 4, comparable to the Self scale. A median under 3 means rework (nothing is cut).

## Dyad: The seam between two adjacent paragraphs inside one chapter, judged as a Third Thing the pair should become.

Draft approved as written by 3 of 10 voters before amendments.

1. **Third Thing Emerges**: Does the pair together say or enable something (a consequence, contrast, test, cost, payoff) that neither paragraph says alone?
   - Yes when: A reader can state something from the pair that neither paragraph states alone, cutting either paragraph weakens the other, and the pair does not merely stack two agreeing claims: at least one paragraph supplies the test, limit, counter-case, or cost the other lacks.
   - No when: The paragraphs could be swapped, merged, or one dropped with nothing lost but length; the pair restates itself or stacks reinforcing claims; or it persuades only through rhythm or parallel phrasing, so that no claim survives once the cadence is removed.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (3 approved the draft as written; 2 proposed the stacking and cadence additions, both adopted).
2. **Handoff Carried**: Does the second paragraph pick up something the first actually established (an example, fact, step, or open tension), so the reader can name what was handed across?
   - Yes when: The second paragraph uses or answers something the first set up on the page, and would read differently if the first were removed.
   - No when: The second paragraph starts cold, re-defines a term just used, leans on a bare connective ('Furthermore', 'Next'), or invokes a capitalized or coined label that the first paragraph only asserted and never earned. Such a label is a stage prop, not a handoff.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (3 approved as written; 1 proposed the stage-prop clause, adopted).
3. **Link Is Honest**: Can the reader name the relation (cause, example, objection, step, reversal, zoom), and is every connective or jump in scope or confidence backed by something on the page?
   - Yes when: The relation is stated or plainly implied; any 'therefore' or 'which is why', any jump from case to rule, and any mechanism claimed (feedback, growth, decay) is backed on the page, with the paragraph showing how sure it is; and the seam does not slide from a described pattern or event to a claim about who the reader is.
   - No when: A tidy or fluent connective covers a non sequitur, so the link feels earned only because it reads smoothly. Also: an anecdote is promoted to a law, an assumption in the first paragraph is stated as settled fact in the second, the seam is dressed in wordplay or hedging instead of stated, or it moves from what the reader does to what the reader is (pattern to identity, lapse to defect).
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (3 approved as written; 4 proposed changes to it, merged into this wording).
4. **Turn Is Even or Earned**: Does the seam hold register, vocabulary, and altitude, or change them visibly and on purpose, while introducing at most one new term or concept across the join?
   - Yes when: Register, term, example, and level of abstraction stay continuous, or the change (story to claim, cosmic to concrete) is prepared by the first paragraph; at most one new idea arrives before the previous one has been used; and a move from pain or personal difficulty to cosmic or systemic framing first acknowledges the hurt.
   - No when: The reader notices an unexplained jump of term or scale, a second new concept lands before the first has been used (a smooth seam that dumps two concepts fails), or a lofty leap lands on a hurting moment without naming the hurt (spiritual bypass).
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (3 approved as written; 4 proposed changes to it, two adopted in full and two folded elsewhere or not adopted).

**Dissent not adopted:** Not adopted: voter 1's wording that makes a named stock, flow, limit, or delay mandatory at every seam (kept only as 'mechanism claimed must be backed'), since most seams claim no mechanism; voter 5's separate plain-join rule (folded into the wordplay clause of Link Is Honest); voter 10's rule that a story-to-claim shift must carry a person or object across, which is too narrow because most seams are not story-to-claim.

## Skein: One chapter: a group of paragraphs bound by a shared aim, the way a Skein is bound by a shared practice.

Draft approved as written by 0 of 10 voters before amendments.

1. **One Stated Aim**: Can a careful reader state in one sentence what this chapter is for, and does the chapter signal that aim near its opening and keep to it?
   - Yes when: Independent readers give essentially the same one-sentence aim, without needing 'and also' or a list of topics.
   - No when: Readers give different aims, the aim only becomes visible at the end, or the title promises something the body does not deliver.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (no one proposed a change).
2. **Every Paragraph Earns**: Does each paragraph visibly serve the aim and add something, so that removing or relocating any one would leave a noticeable gap?
   - Yes when: No paragraph could be lifted into another chapter unchanged, repeat an earlier point at equal strength, or be deleted without loss; where the chapter's claim depends on an idea, someone (a case, face, or cost) stands in it.
   - No when: The chapter has padding, tangents, orphans, or pairs doing the same job twice, including a pile of stacked rhetorical questions or qualifiers where one sentence would carry it, a claim-bearing idea with nobody standing in it, or a paragraph whose only function is to blame or diagnose the reader. The remedy is rework (reduce to the essential sentence, give it a person, describe the pattern the reader is caught in), never silent cutting.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (0 approved as written; 4 proposed additions, all adopted in merged form).
3. **Order Builds to Arrival**: Does the sequence build, each step preparing the next, and end in a landing the body has paid for?
   - Yes when: Shuffling the paragraphs would damage the argument; no concept is used before it is taught; new concepts arrive at a learnable pace (never three at once in one paragraph); and the final paragraph lands on a changed understanding, ideally returning to an image, person, or situation planted near the opening, now carrying more weight.
   - No when: The paragraphs could be shuffled with the argument intact, forward references go unexplained, a difficulty spike or an idle stretch of pure reception appears, the ending restates the opening or stops, or it closes on a confident payoff larger than the body supports (a landing that feels earned from cadence, not from what was shown).
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (0 approved as written; 4 proposed additions, adopted except voter 1's local-rule clause).
4. **Delivers Something Usable**: By the end, can the reader do, notice, or test something specific and small, with a causal account behind it and a sign of whether it worked?
   - Yes when: The reader can name one action, observation, or test small enough to try today in ordinary life, even on a bad day; the chapter supports its claim with a causal account, a worked example with a name or number, or a checkable step (an image alone does not count); and it says what would show the practice is not working or how sure it is, keeping story distinct from evidence.
   - No when: The chapter informs without equipping; gives advice no outcome could contradict ('be present', 'find your loop'); leaves the claim as assertion or metaphor; treats a vivid anecdote as proof; or its main payload is more self-scrutiny or rumination rather than a step.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (0 approved as written; 9 proposed changes to this one, the most contested criterion; the shared core is adopted).

**Dissent not adopted:** Not adopted: voter 4's mandatory named trigger and two-minute version (too rigid for every chapter; kept at World, where the first step is judged); voter 8's requirement that every chapter name whose rules shape the behavior (one voter, and judged at Culture and World where it fits); voter 5's body-only practice (excludes legitimate thought tests); voter 1's local-rule-before-generalization clause (overlaps the causal-account requirement in Delivers Something Usable).

## Culture: One Part of the book (Front Matter, Parts One to Seven, or the Appendix): a set of chapters with its own character and ways, inherited upward from its chapters.

Draft approved as written by 2 of 10 voters before amendments.

1. **Distinct Inherited Character**: Does this Part have a recognizable job, voice, and way of working that differs from its neighbors and visibly comes from what its chapters actually do, rather than from an opener or title imposed on them?
   - Yes when: A reader dropped into a random chapter could guess which Part it belongs to, and the Part's stated promises are matched by what its chapters deliver (no gaps, no orphans).
   - No when: Chapters could be reshuffled into a neighboring Part unnoticed, or the Part opener promises terms and ways the chapters never use.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved the draft as written; no one proposed a change to this criterion).
2. **Chapters Build Each Other**: Do later chapters lean on, extend, or test earlier ones, so the Part shows a result no single chapter reaches alone, with progress the reader can feel inside the Part?
   - Yes when: Removing or reordering a chapter leaves a visible gap and the closing chapter could not be read first without loss; a usable move or payoff arrives before the Part's last chapter; and at least one recurring image, case, or person returns in later chapters with changed weight. Chapters may also qualify or correct one another.
   - No when: The Part is a shelf of standalone essays, chapters repeat the same point with new anecdotes, the order is arbitrary, or all the payoff is deferred to a later Part so the Part reads as setup only.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 3 proposed additions, voters 7 and 10 adopted, voter 1 not).
3. **Ways Held Consistently**: Do the chapters share the same terms, definitions, and standard of evidence, with each use of fractal, loop, or scale earning its meaning, and keep faith with the book's core operation?
   - Yes when: Key terms mean one thing throughout the Part and across Parts; each use of fractal, loop, scale, or repeats names what repeats and what limits it; bolder claims get stronger support than weaker ones; and at least one claim in the Part is exposed to a case or counterexample that could have gone against it, with a report of how it fared.
   - No when: A term shifts meaning between chapters or Parts, or 'fractal' only means deep or pretty; one chapter undercuts another; the largest claims rest on the thinnest support; every cited case confirms the thesis; or a term coined earlier is cited as authority or precedent without having been earned by an example or measurement.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 5 proposed additions, the most of any Culture criterion; voters 1, 6, and 9 adopted).
4. **Honest Entry and Handoff**: Does the Part open by saying what it inherits, close by saying what it did and did not establish, and hand the reader forward with something the next Part uses, without harming a fragile reader?
   - Yes when: The closing claim does not exceed what the chapters supported; limits and costs are named, including who the Part could hurt (rumination, self-blame, use against others) with a way for a fragile reader to pause or step back; the Part speaks to what a person does or is caught in, never to what a person is; and it leaves one repeatable action, not just understanding, which the next Part builds on.
   - No when: The Part ends in a crescendo larger than any chapter supported, trails off or cliff-edges, or is bloated or starved for its role; it asks for deep self-examination or hard truths with no pacing, off-ramp, or acknowledgment of pain; a chapter slides from guilt-type to shame-type framing; or it hands forward ideas only, leaving the reader nothing to run on Monday.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; voters 3 and 4 proposed additions, both adopted).

**Dissent not adopted:** Not adopted: voter 1's requirement that the scale claim be tested at the next level up and down in every Part (covered by the 'what repeats and what limits it' clause, and too heavy for the Front Matter and Appendix); voter 8's rule that structural causes need a named mechanism, owner, or lever (one voter here; adopted at World, where the whole book's reach is judged); voter 3's stance rule was moved from Ways Held Consistently to Honest Entry and Handoff, where it is a safety matter.

## World: The whole book as one thing that lives in a reader's life and meets the world outside its pages.

Draft approved as written by 2 of 10 voters before amendments.

1. **Whole Exceeds Parts and Enacts Itself**: Does the complete book enable something no single Part delivers, with its one operation stated as a mechanism and visibly enacted in its own structure at every scale?
   - Yes when: A reader can name the spine and what the whole adds beyond any Part; the operation is stated as a mechanism (a local rule, what repeats, at what ratio, what limits it) so the structure follows from the thesis; the self-enactment can be checked against the text, not merely claimed by analogy; early images and promises return near the end carrying more weight; and the reader can name at least one person or image they will still remember tomorrow.
   - No when: The thesis is complete in any one Part, the book is a binder of good chapters or of metaphors, the structure would be the same if the thesis were false, enactment is claimed by analogy only so any structure would count, a promise is dropped, or the book ends on a crescendo of abstraction with no human face to pay it off.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 3 proposed additions, voters 1, 6, and 10, all adopted).
2. **Lives In A Life**: Could a reader carry the book into an ordinary week, including a bad one, taking a small first step they can repeat and check, and re-enter the book later without the author or the full worldview?
   - Yes when: A reader can name one practice that takes only a few minutes, the cue that starts it (a trigger, moment, or mental state), what they would do on a bad day when they manage only the smallest version, and how they would know it worked; the step is something the body or attention does and needs no teacher, notebook, or ideal conditions; a lapse is treated as information, not as failure or a character verdict; and there is a way back in.
   - No when: The reader agrees with everything and changes nothing; the first step is large, has no cue, demands energy or self-scrutiny, or works only as a resolution to try harder; a lapse implies the reader is the failing pattern; or the book can be absorbed only linearly, once.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 4 proposed changes to it, voters 3, 4, and 5 adopted, voter 8 moved to Survives Contact).
3. **Survives Contact**: Does the book hold up against a skeptic, a counterexample, a failure of the practice, or a misuse, with stated limits and at least one claim a hostile but fair reader could in principle show wrong?
   - Yes when: The book states where its central claim stops helping and what evidence would count against it; names at least one case where the loop does not hold or does not scale up or down; states which problems personal practice cannot fix because incentives, institutions, or environments produce them, and does not let inner work stand in for shared action; and gives the strongest opposing view in the voice of someone who holds it for good reasons.
   - No when: Every counterexample is absorbed as another instance of the pattern; the framework explains everything and so predicts nothing; opponents appear as straw men; inner work is offered as the remedy for something designed into the environment, or fatalism excuses the reader from acting; or the book is a closed system only believers can use.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 3 proposed additions, voters 1, 8, and 10, all adopted).
4. **Trustworthy and Safe in the Hands**: Does the book keep its voice honest about what it knows and stay safe for the most vulnerable reader and for use on other people, working as an offer of help rather than a verdict or label?
   - Yes when: The book's total confidence ends no higher than its support; key terms stay stable; it says plainly which claims are measured or checkable and which are metaphor or useful fiction; it tells a reader in acute distress to seek human or professional help; every route through it leaves a fragile reader no worse off; and its vocabulary resists being used as an accusation or diagnosis.
   - No when: The book finishes more certain than it began; it drifts on central terms; it wears its ritual costume with a straight face and no wink toward the reader; it implies a reader's suffering is caused by their own beliefs or loops, or bypasses pain with oneness or acceptance language before acknowledging it; or it offers blame or diagnosis of who the reader is or hands out language easy to weaponize against self or others.
   - Support: 10 of 10 backed it (2 approved as written; 3 proposed additions, voters 3 and 9 adopted, voter 5 not).

**Dissent not adopted:** Not adopted: voter 5's 'calm to read aloud' and 'wordier than its content needs' tests for Trustworthy and Safe (too subjective for a yes/no answer, and wordiness is judged at the Skein scale); voter 8's requirement that the first step feed shared action (merged into Survives Contact, where the limits of personal practice are judged, to keep Lives In A Life about the first step); voter 4's fixed two-minute limit and 'one question to carry' (replaced with 'a few minutes' and left optional).
