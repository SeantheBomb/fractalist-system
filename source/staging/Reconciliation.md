## A. The Working Mantras, tested against the Standing Thirds (2026-10-08)

**Status:** parked. Sean, 2026-10-08: the Standing Thirds are still in development, so this result is provisional and should be re-run when the catalogue settles. One rater (Claude), one pass.

### The question

Can a handful of working rules generate the catalogue, so that nobody has to memorise fifty-two figures? The test: for each figure, the Tending pole should read as one mantra applied at that figure's scale, and the Fraying pole as that mantra broken or applied at the wrong size.

### The five as proposed

| Key | Mantra | Instruction |
|---|---|---|
| A | Tend the act | Change the small repeated thing, not the outcome |
| B | Tend the between | Fix how two sides translate each other, not the people |
| L | Walk the loop | Trace it round to where it started |
| M | Mind what adds up | Settle what can cancel; be deliberate about what only accumulates |
| S | Check the size | A change of scale changes what the same act builds |

Guard, applied to the mantras themselves: **find the other pole.**

### Result

| | Fits | One pole only | No fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-Kin (12) | 5 | 2 | 5 |
| Dyad-Kin (19) | 11 | 6 | 2 |
| Skein-Kin (11) | 10 | 1 | 0 |
| Culture-Kin (7) | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| World-Kin (3) | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| **Total (52)** | **30** | **13** | **9** |

The Infinite Boundary has no poles and is outside the test by design.

**The five are not comprehensive.** Twenty of the twenty-two misses share one cause: a stand-in has come loose from what it stands for. The stand-in is a picture (The Mask, The Keepsake, Filling the Gap), a measure (The Mark, Proxy Detachment, The Standing Account), or a means (Gaming the Terms, Connection First, The Guard). None of the five says to check it.

### The sixth

| Key | Mantra | Instruction | Blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | Check the stand-in | Ask whether the picture, the measure or the means still matches what it stands for | Checking without end. The Tested Price's Fraying pole is exactly this: "the testing has become the relationship" |

It is derivable from the same rule as the others. "No frame is the correct one" was only ever turned outward, between two people (B). Turned inward, between a person and the world, it gives T.

With T added: 50 of 52 fit. The Mirror and The Namer remain loose fits on B and A.

### Other findings

- **No mantra is redundant.** M and L travel together often (Escalation, Typecasting, The Sagging Floor) but separate cleanly: The Tide is M with no loop; The Echo is L with nothing accumulating.
- **S is thin.** Three primary fits, all at Skein scale or above. Expected, since size cannot matter before there is scale, but it is the mantra most at risk of being a special case of M.
- **The named blind spots are real poles.** M's blind spot was "keeping score"; The Shared Pot's Tending pole is trust that makes accounting unnecessary, and its Fraying pole is nobody protecting the pot.
- **The largest family was the one missed.** T covers twenty figures; the next largest, M, covers ten. The five were derived from physics, which has no observer who can mistake a picture for the thing.

### Limits of this test

- One rater, who also wrote the mantras. A second reading should be done by someone with no stake in the count.
- Short mantras stretch. "Fits" was held to: the Tending line is recognisable as the mantra's own instruction. A looser standard would have passed nearly everything and shown nothing.
- The source shelf has not been checked.

### Two missing mantras

Both tests fail in the same two places.

| Key | Mantra | Instruction | Blind spot (already in Part Two) |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | Watch it run | See the pattern while it is running, before touching it | Watching in place of acting. Day 7: "did naming any of it change what you actually did?" |
| R | Make it worth repeating | Ask whether you would repeat exactly this, unchanged, indefinitely, and whether it is yours or owed | A sophisticated way to quit. Ch 7: every hard stretch reclassified as a want problem |

W comes first in practice: Part Two closes by saying every practice in it trains noticing a loop from inside it. R is the only mantra that says which way to point the others; the first six are all mechanics.

The existing devotional mantras already hold both: "You feel a line. You are a loop. You build a tree." (W) and "Let today's small step be worth repeating" (R). The two sets are complementary, not rivals.

### Why the six missed them

The six were derived from the physics of interaction. They cover what happens between things and say nothing about attention or value, which have no counterpart in a field theory. The same bias showed in section A, where Self-Kin was the weakest tier.

### Findings from the shelf

- **Confirmation from books newer than the manuscript.** The note "Coordination Requires Shared Frame" (Ostrom, Schelling) is B at group scale.
- **A real tension with Ch 19.** The manuscript says a Skein that starts keeping score is drifting toward a Banner. Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (passage #25992), finds that in every long-enduring commons "active investments in monitoring and sanctioning activities are quite apparent", done by the participants themselves. S resolves it: uncounted trust holds at Skein size and fails at commons size. Ch 19 does not say so.
- **A lead, not verified.** A shelf note reports that *It's on You* (Chater and Loewenstein) argues individual-level fixes draw attention away from changing the rules. If the passages bear that out, it is the strongest source-level challenge to A. No passage was opened.
- **S stays thin.** A supporting fit in four chapters, the primary fit in none. Candidate to fold into M ("and what it adds up to at the next size"), which would hold the set at seven.

### Limits

- One rater, who also wrote the mantras.
- Shelf scoring is by note title and summary; one passage was opened.
- Parts One and Three to Seven were not tested.

### Proposed change, if approved

Add a section of working mantras beside the devotional ones: W, A, B, L, M, T, R, with S either kept or folded into M, each with its blind spot, and the guard "find the other pole". No existing text changes. Separately: one sentence in Ch 19 on where uncounted kindness stops scaling.

## C. The derivation map (2026-10-08)

**Status:** built, proposed. Sean approved the framing (a formal system of taking part in patterns you cannot see whole) and asked for a map of relations in place of linear prose.

- The map is `source/Derivation_Map.json`: five axioms with passage-level sources, three rules of production, nine mantras, and one trace per chapter. It supersedes the mantra tables in sections A and B.
- `npm run reconcile` checks it against the manuscript. `npm run reconcile -- --accept` records the current text of each traced chapter as reviewed. It runs before every build.
- The mantras are theorems, not axioms. The Leverage is a theorem too, as the Four Pillars chapter already says.
- All 63 traceable chapters are traced: 6 foundations, 2 alphabet, 6 interpretations, 45 derived practices, 4 partial. Part Five and five tables keyed to its figures are parked.
- Corrections from the full trace. "Frames are private" is stated in seven chapters, starting with The Loop Runs You Right Now; it is just not named as a pillar. "Worth is the measure" is the Seventh Iteration. "Check the size" is the second most used mantra (22 chapters), so the earlier suggestion to fold it into "Mind what adds up" is withdrawn.
- Open, for Sean:
  1. Chance: admitted as the sixth axiom (Sean, 2026-10-09), sourced from five books.
  2. "Frames are private" as a pillar: not yet (Sean, 2026-10-09). He wants a far better account of what it means first, and holds pillars to the standard of Loop, Nest, emergence and Leverage. Later the same day he renamed it Perspective and placed it as derived from the pillars. The map now holds it as a theorem of the first three axioms, beside Leverage; the first axiom gained the word "local" to carry the derivation. Whether a theorem with Leverage's standing is a pillar is his to decide.
  3. The four partial chapters each name something no mantra reaches: help from others (The Compost Heap), variation as immunity (Fractal Resistance), a one-way choice (What a Weaving Cannot Show), the cost of keeping order (Anchoring).
  4. What a Turning Is says a designed thing has an aim, not two poles. The guard applies two poles to the mantras, which are designed things.

- Pillars trimmed to three (Sean, 2026-10-09): Loop, Third Thing, Nest. Not yet applied to the manuscript, where "The Four Pillars" is a chapter title and sits on the Quick Reference Card. Sean wants the relationship between the pillars and the pair (Leverage, Perspective) made clear through the pillar metaphor before anything is rewritten. Proposal on the table: the three pillars hold up a place to stand; Leverage and Perspective are what any one position on it gives you, reach and view.
- Standpoint accepted as the working name for Leverage and Perspective (Sean, 2026-10-09). Proposal on the table for Chance and Worth: they are the two things the pillars leave open, one from each side. The world brings chance; you bring worth. In loop terms the Standpoint is a loop between a person and the pattern, with Perspective as its inbound arrow, Leverage as its outbound arrow, Worth as what it is aimed at, and Chance as what disturbs it.
