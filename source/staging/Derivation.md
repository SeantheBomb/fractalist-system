### The question

Can a handful of working rules generate the catalogue, so that nobody has to memorise fifty-two figures? The test: for each figure, the Tending pole should read as one mantra applied at that figure's scale, and the Fraying pole as that mantra broken or applied at the wrong size.

|---|---|---|

| L | Walk the loop | Trace it round to where it started |
| M | Mind what adds up | Settle what can cancel; be deliberate about what only accumulates |

- **No mantra is redundant.** M and L travel together often (Escalation, Typecasting, The Sagging Floor) but separate cleanly: The Tide is M with no loop; The Echo is L with nothing accumulating.

- **The named blind spots are real poles.** M's blind spot was "keeping score"; The Shared Pot's Tending pole is trust that makes accounting unnecessary, and its Fraying pole is nobody protecting the pot.

## E. The Standing Thirds as a formal system (2026-10-09)

**Status:** analysis and proposal. Nothing in Part Five is changed.

### What the catalogue already is

A small formal system: twelve Self-Kin as givens, one production rule (fuse), forty derived figures. Run mechanically against the site's own data, counting a figure fused with itself as allowed:

| Tier | Figures | Pairs the rule allows | Share used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dyad | 19 | 78 | 24% |
| Skein | 11 | 61 (two Dyad-Kin sharing a Self-Kin) | 18% |
| Culture | 7 | 17 | 41% |
| World | 3 | 12 (5 if a figure cannot fuse with itself) | 25% |

### Where it falls short of one

1. **No rule says which fusions fail.** The manuscript says the unused pairs fail "the Third Thing test". That test is a judgment. So the rule lists candidates and a person picks the figures.
2. **The givens have nothing under them.** Self-Kin are introduced with "there is nothing underneath them". The map now puts axioms, three pillars, the Standpoint and the open sides underneath everything else.
3. **Derived is being read as real.** Culture-Kin and World-Kin are flagged as three and four inference-layers deep. Being producible by the rule and being observed in the world are separate claims, and the catalogue records only the first.
4. **Poles are authored per figure.** A fused figure's Tending and Fraying are written by hand, not derived from its parents'.
5. **The rule is stated unevenly.** Self-fusion is allowed at Dyad tier (Spark with Spark) and used once at Culture tier (Independent Convergence) but nowhere else. The World-Kin chapter says the rule "produces three results and no others"; that holds only if Independent Convergence is excluded, which the text does not say. With it, five pairs share a Skein-Kin.
6. **Use is lopsided.** The Spark is in six Dyad fusions; The Salve, The Easier Door, The Riverbed and The Keepsake are in one each.

### Proposed reshaping

1. **Derive the Self-Kin.** Each should be a theorem of the axioms: one way a single person's loop with the pattern runs. The four parts of the Standpoint loop (Perspective, Leverage, Worth, Chance) are the place to start.
2. **Build the tier in pairs.** If fraying is a split pair, every Self-Kin has a partner whose absence is its Fraying. Then poles are derived, not authored, and the two-pole test becomes "name the partner".
3. **Two marks on every figure:** derived (the rule produces it) and observed (a source or a life shows it). Derived and unobserved is a prediction, the only kind of result that can test the system. Observed and underivable is an orphan, which points at a missing rule.
4. **State the whole rule,** including self-fusion and what "sharing a component" means, so anyone can list every candidate.
5. **Keep the judgment and say where it is.** Listing candidates can be mechanical. Deciding which are real cannot. The four tests stay as that filter.
6. **Keep the faces.** A name is a chunk: it installs faster than a derivation. The derivation sits underneath it.

### Order of work this implies

Poles vocabulary (D.9) first, then Self-Kin pairs, then the rule, then regenerate the upper tiers and compare with the fifty-two.

## F. Weavings, reworked through GEB (2026-10-09)

**Status:** proposal. Assumption: no record of an earlier redesign exists in this repo or in past sessions, so the open problem is taken from the manuscript itself: What a Weaving Cannot Show says there is no layout for a one-way choice, and the deck depends on a catalogue that is still moving.

### The reading

GEB describes a canon as one theme copied with changes that keep all its information: moved in pitch, slowed or sped up, turned upside down, played backward. A fugue brings one subject in voice by voice.

| Canon move | Weaving already has |
|---|---|
| Change of pitch | The ring: the same pattern at another scale |
| Change of speed | Weather, Climate, Terrain |
| Inversion | Tending and Fraying |
| Backward | Nothing yet |
| One subject, many voices | The Full Unravelling |

### What this exposes

- **The Four-Point is not one theme in four voices.** Its four cards are drawn independently, so the rings are four unrelated subjects. The Full Unravelling is the true fugue: one subject, each further voice derived.
- **The two spreads do different jobs,** and the Standpoint names them. Independent draws force positions you would not have chosen: that is Perspective. Derived draws show a small thing becoming a large one, step by step: that is Leverage.
- **The rings are a stack.** GEB's push and pop: go down a level without losing your place, and come back. The Full Unravelling only goes up.
- **A miss narrows; it does not decide.** GEB's figure and ground: knowing what something is not does not always tell you what it is.

### Proposed forms

| Form | How it is drawn | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| The Single Pull | One card | Unchanged |
| The Four-Point | Four independent cards, one per ring | More positions (Perspective) |
| The Full Unravelling | Two Self-Kin drawn, the rest derived upward | Where a small thing becomes large (Leverage) |
| **The Tracing** (new) | One large figure chosen, then walked down through its parents to a Self-Kin | Which act of mine feeds this. Tracing the Fold, as a spread |
| **The Fork** (new) | One root, two candidate rules, each derived up through the scales side by side | A one-way choice. Fills the gap the manuscript names |

### Proposed reading moves, usable on any card

- **Change the ring:** what is this at the next scale up or down?
- **Change the speed:** Weather, Climate or Terrain?
- **Name the partner:** which pole is missing? (Replaces deciding Tending or Fraying "by context".)
- **Read it backward:** how does the other person tell this? The Second Pass, as a move.
- **Close the ring:** how does the outermost card set the conditions for the innermost? The rings are nested; this makes them a loop.

### Depends on

Section E. The Tracing and the Fork both need the fusion rule stated in full, and "name the partner" needs the Self-Kin pairs.

### What survives from D to F, and why

- **Three ideas, three words.** Direction (Tending or Fraying, per L1). Balance (when two patterns meet: tightens, loosens or held, per L4). Worth (whether it is a lifeline or a snare). The table in D.9 (forcing, watching, entitlement, fatalism) describes balance, not Fraying. Value comes from the Worth axiom, which is why the rope can be either.
- **The generators are the canon; the figures are a library.** On T1: neither 52 nor 125 should be canonical as a list. Canon is the ranks, the sizes and the composition rules. Named figures are what those produce, kept with their evidence marks. This is the elegance standard applied to the catalogue.
- **Two routes to the ranks.** The recap's merge test would reduce the catalogue to ranks from the bottom up. Deriving ranks from the axioms comes from the top down. Where the two agree is the strongest result available, by the manuscript's own standard for independent arrival.
- **Predictions and orphans.** A first sketch maps the nine ranks onto the axioms: Spark and Tide to the two loop types, Story, Gardener and Mirror to Perspective, Salve and Riverbed to Leverage, Mark to Worth, Spillover to the Nest. Nothing maps to Chance. Either a rank is missing or chance is not a pattern a person runs. Unverified.

- **Three rings (T5).** GEB notes the number of levels is a choice of resolution. Three is the fewest that shows the Nest and its gate: you and the world meet only through what is close. The book can keep finer scales.
- **A missing step in weave6.** Its steps cover chance (the deal), Perspective (how it stands), composition (where they meet) and Leverage (where to go). Nothing asks whether the pattern is worth repeating.

## I. The formal system, in its four slots (2026-10-09)

**Status:** applied to the map (version 2). The manuscript is unchanged. Sean, same day: the formal system is still the backbone; the pillars are not its axioms.

### The correction

Sections B to G called the descriptive claims "axioms" and the instructions "theorems". In a formal system axioms and theorems are the same kind of thing: a theorem is whatever the rules produce from the axioms. If the theorems are practices, the axioms have to be moves. The descriptive claims were never axioms. They are what the system means.

### The slots

| Slot | Holds |
|---|---|
| Alphabet | The Loop Tongue: chains of arrows, closed into loops, bracketed when they hold, tagged with a scale |
| Axioms | Four moves: **look, plant, tend, thin** |
| Rules | At a scale. With another. In order (look first) |
| Theorems | The practices: every chapter that tells you what to do |
| Ground (the meaning) | The one operation, the three pillars, the Standpoint, direction, chance and worth |
| Guides | The former nine mantras: advice about which move to make |

The four moves are the manuscript's own. The Daily Practices: "Look, plant, tend, thin. That is the whole of it, and everything else in this part is those four moves at a larger scale or with another person in the room." That sentence already states axioms and two rules of production. They also cover every change that can be made to a loop: read it, add one, raise its strength, lower it.

### The test

Every practice chapter was traced to the moves it is made of. Part Five and the tables keyed to it stay parked.

| | |
|---|---|
| Practice chapters | 49 |
| Fully made of the four moves | 47 |
| Partly | 2 (Receiving; Fractal Resistance) |

| Move | Practices using it | Self | Dyad | Skein | Culture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Look | 44 | 29 | 13 | 10 | 1 |
| Plant | 16 | 6 | 5 | 6 | 0 |
| Tend | 12 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 1 |
| Thin | 8 | 6 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

### What the test shows

1. **The book mostly teaches looking.** 24 of 49 practices are made of Look alone. All of Part Three and all of Part Six are Look.
2. **Thin is nearly absent beyond the self.** One practice at dyad scale, one at skein. The book says how to start and sustain things with other people and almost never how to weaken or end one.
3. **Culture scale has almost no practice.** Two entries, and one is a comet.
4. **One thing is not a move: deliberately not moving.** Receiving lists "doing nothing" among its six responses. Fractal Resistance has "accept". Onboarding Day 1 is "change nothing". It recurs, the manuscript treats it as a skill, and none of the four covers it. Either it is a fifth axiom or it is what Look is when nothing follows.
5. **Worth is not a move.** "Would I repeat exactly this?" is a judgment the system does not produce. That is expected: it is the meaning read into the system from outside.

### Limits

- One rater, who chose the four moves and then scored against them. Short verbs stretch.
- Chapters are large. A chapter marked "look, plant" contains several practices; the trace is per chapter, not per practice.
- Thin has the weakest shelf support of the four: three books, one passage each, and one of those is shared with Tend.

### Open

- A fifth move for not moving, or not.
- Whether to trace per practice instead of per chapter.
- The guides overlap the moves (Watch it run is Look). They may reduce to a short list of cautions.
- Part Five: under this structure the figures are not theorems. They are named patterns, which is to say words in the alphabet. That changes what the catalogue is for and needs its own pass.

## J. The catalogue in the formal system (2026-10-11)

Words, not theorems. A figure is a named pattern: a loop that can be written in the Loop Tongue and recognised by name. Three things follow.
