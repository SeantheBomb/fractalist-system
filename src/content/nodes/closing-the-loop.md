Three movements, three scales, and one thing worth saying plainly now that you've moved through all of them: they were never actually separate. Self, Dyad, and Skein are the same Nest, examined at different resolutions — which means a practice that only ever holds at one of these scales isn't actually finished yet, however solid it feels in isolation.

Suffering runs through all three the same way, worth naming once here rather than expanding on: it's often the friction that reveals what a scale needed and wasn't getting. Held with the distance Chapter 4 already taught you — pain observed rather than pain obeyed — it converts into something usable instead of curdling into bitterness. The capacity to make that conversion has a name, hope, and it's trainable the same way everything else here is: not a temperament some people are born with more of, but a loop, practiced. Whatever gets built this way doesn't stay contained at Self scale, any more than a <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/local-rule/">Local Rule</a> stays contained to the loop you first thought you were running. It travels, the same way everything real in this book travels.

This is the whole shape of Part Two, said once more, all the way through: you tend the Self, because an untended Self sends its chaos outward. You bring something worth magnifying into a Dyad, because a Dyad amplifies whatever you actually bring it, not what you meant to bring. You let a Skein form around real, repeated contact rather than a label, because a Skein built on anything else was never going to hold weight. And the whole practice, done honestly at any one of these scales, eventually — filtered, delayed, imperfectly, through whatever Fold it has to travel — comes home to all three at once.

**The practices in this part are not a list, and it's worth seeing why before you close it.** Naming a loop makes you able to catch a self-story, because a self-story is only a loop you hadn't noticed was one. Catching a self-story is what makes the Second Pass — the Loop Tongue practice waiting in Part Three — possible, because you can't say the second half of a conflict honestly while still believing your version of yourself is simply the truth. Doing the Second Pass with one person is what makes a Dyad durable enough to be worth having. A durable Dyad is what gives you something real to bring into a Skein. And a Skein that actually functions hands you back the one thing you could never get alone: other people's honest read on the loops you're still running, which is where the next naming starts.

That's a closed loop, not a checklist — the last practice feeding the first, which is why nothing here has a completion state. You don't finish the Self movement and graduate to the Dyad one. You run all three, badly at first, and each one makes the others slightly more possible than they were last month. If the practices ever start feeling like a sequence you're working through, you've turned a complex thing back into a complicated one, and you'll get the results a checklist gets.

None of this was ever a plan for self-improvement measured in isolation, and none of it was ever a plan for building something bigger than yourself either. It's smaller and stranger than both: learning to help yourself in ways that happen to help the people around you, and trusting the rest to a rising tide rather than a ledger. You don't lift anyone by managing the water level. You just stop holding your own boat down, and let what happens next actually happen.

**And one thing more, said plainly, because everything in this part has been one long argument for it.**

You are planting every day whether you mean to or not. Nothing in this book starts the planting — every repeated thought, every sentence said in a particular tone, every small thing done again is already going into the ground. The only thing any of this changes is whether you can see what you are putting there. And seeing it is what makes available a choice that was not available before: plant out of care rather than out of fear.

Let what you are aiming at hold both the fast things and the slow ones. The fast things are what keep you standing while the slow ones are still underground, and the slow ones are what you were actually growing. Whatever fails becomes compost, and compost is not a consolation prize — it is the reason the next planting starts from better ground than the last.

Tend yourself, your Dyads, your Skein, the culture you are inside of, the world that culture is inside of. Not because any of it owes you a return — it does not, and anyone promising one is selling something. Tend them because the Nest runs both ways, and a forest tends whoever is standing in it. Some seasons that will be you. Other seasons it will be someone you never met, standing in shade you spent years growing and will never be thanked for.

What consistency buys is not an outcome. **It is a canopy.** And a canopy takes the light without aiming it — it changes what can take root underneath it, so that some things find the conditions easy and others never get purchase, including some you would have wanted: a mature commitment shades out options that were live before it. *(The shading is ecology, and the version about your own life is offered as a resemblance rather than demonstrated — it is what this book means by a model, and it should be held the way models are held.)* That is the whole of the leverage available here. It is considerably more than it sounds like.

What every practice in this part has actually been training is not a set of tricks for feeling better, but the underlying capacity to notice a loop while you're still inside it and reach in anyway — a capacity most of your life runs entirely without, by default, and one this book has never claimed comes standard. That noticing, exercised on purpose, at whatever scale it's needed, is what this practice was asking of you all along.

You don't finish this part of the book. You close the loop, and it starts again, at whichever scale your actual life is asking for next.
