**How to read the gaps.** The first four columns are full; World and Cosmos carry the open cells. Cosmos is sparse by design: there is only one totality, so there can only be one Third standing at that scale. The remaining open cells may eventually fill with practices rather than figures. A new figure is added only when it predicts, compresses, or tells you where to push — completeness is not a virtue here, and filling a cell for its own sake is complication wearing a complexity costume.

### Concepts, and where to find them

**The core mantra** — *You feel a line. You are a loop. You build a tree.* Pt.1 Ch.3 · Pt.2 Ch.4 · Pt.3 Ch.1, 5 · Pt.4 Ch.2

**Reading a system — the two questions** — First: does anything here feed back into itself? Second: which order am I looking at? A complex system also predicts its own skew — feedback concentrates outcomes rather than spreading them evenly, which is the real mechanism behind the familiar 80/20 observation. Pt.1 Ch.8–9 · Pt.2 Ch.9 · Pt.4 Ch.3 · Pt.6 Ch.10

**Complicated vs. complex** — The first question. No feedback, no Third Thing, no acceleration to read. Pt.1 Ch.8 · Pt.4 Ch.3 · Pt.6 Ch.10

**Position, speed, acceleration** — The second question. You never touch position directly; you apply a force, which is acceleration, and time integrates the rest. Why high-leverage work shows no visible movement for a long time. Pt.1 Ch.9 · Pt.2 Ch.5, 9, 11 · Pt.4 Ch.3

**Elegance** — Maximum complexity from minimum complication. Pt.1 Ch.8 · Pt.2 Ch.3, 9

**The three pillars, and what they hold up** — One operation seen three times: a loop that holds becomes a part. Pt.1 Ch.4

**The Standpoint** — Leverage and Perspective: your reach and your view, the two directions of one loop between you and the pattern. What the pillars make unavoidable for anyone standing inside them. Pt.1 Ch.4 · Pt.2 Ch.10 · Pt.4 Ch.7

**Chance and worth** — What the pillars leave open. The world brings the first; you bring the second. Pt.1 Ch.4, 10 · Pt.2 Ch.11

**The four moves, and hold** — Look, plant, tend, thin: every change that can be made to a loop. Hold is tending and thinning at once. Pt.2 opening · Pt.2 Ch.2 · Pt.7 Ch.0

**The Woven Self** — The Third Thing pointed at the self. Pt.1 Ch.7 · Pt.2 Ch.4 · Pt.4 Ch.2

**The symphysical, and emergence** — What physical things make once their parts interact, standing beside "metaphysical" rather than above it. Emergence has a threshold (phase transition) and a direction (weak, in-principle traceable, vs. strong, not traceable even in principle). Pt.1 Ch.2, 8 · Pt.4 Ch.3

**Emergence vs. luck; "luck = preparation + time"** — Chance is a fact about one instant; emergence is a fact about the shape many instants make together. A good Local Rule improves the distribution of outcomes across many attempts — it never guarantees the one you're currently on. The popular equation overclaims on both signs: the "=" should be probabilistic, and the "+" should be "×," since captured luck compounds future exposure rather than simply adding to it. Pt.1 Ch.10 (Genesis, Fourth Iteration) · Pt.2 Ch.11 (The Sacrifice Trap)

**Coasting vs. accelerating — why the same practice costs some people everything and others almost nothing** — Holding a speed already reached costs only enough to cancel drag; building it from zero costs far more, because one is maintaining existing momentum and the other is generating momentum that doesn't exist yet. Explains why people quit mid-build, mistaking the cost of construction for evidence of failure. Test: has this gotten easier, or have you just gotten used to how hard it is? Pt.2 Ch.8

**Three kinds of knowing** — Being told a pattern (the Loop-name, transmissible, cheap), having lived a pattern (its Tree, which can only be walked), and knowing which pattern you're currently inside (neither of the first two, and not produced by either). Scale-Tagging, the complex/complicated gate, and the two-pole check all serve the third. Pt.3 Ch.4

**Solving for the third leg** — Your Self-Kin, theirs, and the Dyad-Kin you produce are three linked elements; knowing two points toward the third, though lived patterns are less tidy than the table, so the solve is a reading rather than a lookup. The useful direction: you can't change their leg, but changing yours changes what you produce together. Pt.5 Ch.2

**Mutuality, not accounting** — Forces come in pairs; the force lives in the interaction, not in either object. You cannot affect without being affected. But equal-and-opposite applies to forces, not outcomes — mutual does not mean zero-sum, and almost nothing worth caring about is conserved. Pt.1 Ch.2

**Balance is a wave** — Balance is never stillness; it is continuous correction whose average looks like rest. Stopping the correction is a distinct failure from retreating early. If you can't see the work, that's your resolution, not the thing. Pt.2 Ch.6 · Pt.5 Ch.1

**Isomorphism, and the Nest's falsification test** — Same structure, different labels. The formal content of "the same pattern at another scale" — and therefore a demand: show the correspondence part for part, or admit it's a resemblance. Separate components with no path between them is why independent arrival counts as evidence at all. Pt.4 Ch.3

**Distortion scales with distance** — The Fold returns nearly intact from one step away and nearly unreadable from six, because each edge crossed adds its own shaping. Pt.2 Ch.12

**Fusion depth** — A Third's scale is what it is made of, not a category assigned to it: two selves running currents produce a dyad-scale Third; two dyads sharing a person produce a skein. The tiers of Part Five are composition, not ranking. Pt.5 Ch.0–5

**The two-pole rule** — Every running Third carries a Tending pole, where it runs stronger, and a Fraying pole, where it runs weaker; neither is good or evil in itself. If only one pole is visible, the zoom is too tight — widen until both appear. The Infinite Boundary has none by the same rule widened: a pole is read from somewhere outside, and there is no outside there; a stopped pattern has none either, since nothing is running. Pt.5 Ch.0, 6

**Weather, Climate, Terrain** — The three speeds at which any drawn figure can be read: now, this season, the shape underneath a life. Distinct from ring position, which names how many people a pattern runs through rather than how long it has been running. Distress reliably makes Weather feel like Terrain. Pt.6 Ch.4

**The Long Visitor** — The Cosmos-scale Turning, triggered by a great comet rather than any calendar: the largest possible gathering, everywhere at once, outdoors. Builds the species briefly as a single witnessing thing. One restraint — celebrate the witnessing, refuse the omen. Pt.7 Ch.6

**Solidity as a Third Thing** — You are not touching your chair; no particle in you meets a particle in it, and what you feel as resistance is a field relationship with real emptiness on both sides. Pushed to its physical limit, this is the clearest possible case that a relationship can be as real as the things it relates. Pt.1 Ch.2

**Open and closed boundaries; models** — A closed boundary (judged by external behavior, not internal accuracy) is not a lesser Word — it's often the correct one, and the elegance test applies directly: the right boundary gives the most correct prediction for the least internal machinery the purpose actually needs. A closed boundary corrodes into a Label only once it's kept past the point it stops predicting anything, or kept unopened once a genuinely different purpose needs what's inside. The same act, in different language, as naming a scale. Pt.3 Ch.2 · Pt.4 Ch.6

**Atomic composition and the Woven Self** — Electrons move constantly at no cost to identity (Numbers-level); neutrons change the mass but not the element, and some combinations are unstable, decaying on their own schedule (echoes the Compost Heap's unprocessed weight); changing the proton count takes a nuclear reaction and changes the element itself (Paradigm-level). A physical body for the claim that a self changes constantly at one cost and almost never at another. Pt.4 Ch.2

**Two senses of "real"** — Real as structure (a Third Thing is real independent of anyone perceiving it — solidity, a floral electric field) and real as occurrence (a feeling's occurrence is real simply because it's happening, separate from whether its content is true). Confusing the two is a major source of unnecessary internal struggle. Pt.1 Ch.2 · Pt.2 Ch.4

**Floral electric fields** — A dynamically updating relational field, altered by every visit and read by the next visitor for real information neither party holds alone; also the Fold's mechanism with no metaphor required, since each visitor reads a field already shaped by everyone before them. Pt.1 Ch.2 · Pt.2 Ch.12

**Constraint counting, and which unknown to decide first** — Facing several genuinely tangled unknowns (a budget, a deadline, the Iron Triangle), decide whichever one reality has already constrained the most, not whichever feels most exciting to decide. Pt.2 Ch.9

**Sign-flipping couplings** — Distinct from an ordinary threshold crossing: a single relationship whose effective sign changes as a direct consequence of crossing a threshold, same rule, no new force (logistic growth's carrying capacity; debt, expansionary below a capacity and corrosive above it, general shape credible, specific universal thresholds not). Worse than ordinary burnout, since the behavior doesn't change to announce the reversal. Pt.2 Ch.11

**Interpersonal complementarity** — Warmth invites warmth, coldness invites coldness (correspondence); dominance invites deference and deference invites dominance (complementarity, opposite answered with opposite). Two different mechanisms on two different axes, not one universal rule. Pt.2 Ch.14

**The Oriental hornet's solar cycle** — A literal, not metaphorical, instance of tide-based homeostasis: solar energy harvested by the cuticle, activity correlated with sunlight intensity, the same reinforcing-then-balancing rhythm already claimed for the tide, running in a real insect. Pt.2 Ch.6

**Signed-loop polarity** — Every link in a loop carries a sign (does more of the cause produce more or less of the effect); count the negative links, and an even count (including zero) is reinforcing, an odd count is balancing. The actual arithmetic underneath the ↻ and ⤸ marks — derivable, not a judgment call. Pt.3 Ch.3

**The Nest, read downward** — The pillar stated in both directions. A Global Form at one scale is a Local Rule at the next going *down* as surely as up: a Culture's form is a rule running in an ordinary day, a Skein's is a rule running inside a Dyad. Consequence, and the reason it matters: a Local Rule found in yourself has two possible origins — built by repetition, or delivered — which feel identical from inside and answer to different interventions. Pt.1 Ch.4 · Pt.2 Ch.3 · Pt.4 Ch.3

**Mine to work, not my fault** — Two questions that are easy to collapse into one. If rules arrive from above as a matter of course, then a rule you did not choose, installed by conditions you did not set, is still yours to work and was never yours to answer for. Dropping the first is helplessness; dropping the second is self-blame. Pt.2 Ch.3

**Two gates** — The ladder is gated by position; the Nest is gated by connection. Both are gates — the Nest is not free, since by the catalogue's fusion rule every scale above Self is built out of dyads, so a person with none is outside the Nest rather than at the bottom of it. The asymmetry is that they open from different sides: position changes when someone else decides, connection changes when you do. Hence the first dyad is a change in kind rather than in amount. Pt.2 Ch.10

**Reach is composable** — *Unreachable* is not a property of a rung but a relation between a rung and whoever reaches. The three options of the power gap were computed with the actor fixed at one person; the Third Thing pillar already says the actor is composable. And one person's act travels by being seen — it becomes an input to other people's Local Rules — which is why a small act done visibly differs in kind from the same act done privately. Pt.2 Ch.10 · Pt.2 Ch.1 (Day 7)

**The Fold is the Nest seen up close** — One circuit described in two places: a rule travelling out and returning changed is the Fold; a form above arriving as a rule in your day is the Nest read downward. The circuit only closes once the Nest runs both ways. It closes at two speeds — Dyad into Skein is observable within a life, Skein into Culture is not — which is why the honest statement is that you tend the ground rather than your own return. Pt.2 Ch.12

**The ledger error, both directions** — *I tended, so good is owed* and *bad happened, so something was frayed* are one mistake: an account inferred from an outcome. The forward version is harder to catch because it feels like faith rather than blame. Pt.2 Ch.12

**Fun as pattern absorption** — From game design, and distinct from challenge-skill balance rather than duplicating it: balance says where the sweet spot sits, absorption says what the pleasure signals — that a pattern is going in. Gives the practice a prediction: if it has stopped feeling like anything, either it has been absorbed and a harder one is due, or it was never teaching a pattern. Pt.4 Ch.4

**The rubber hand** — The self's boundary demonstrated rather than argued: two agreeing sensory streams relocate body ownership onto an object within about a minute, complete with protective flinch. Stated narrowly — it shows the minimal self is an assembled model that updates on evidence, not that character is arbitrary. Pt.4 Ch.2

**The two halves of one famous number** — The worked case for appeal-versus-measurement. Leaf placement advancing a near-constant fraction of a turn is measurable and has a reason. The same proportion as a universal law of beauty is largely one nineteenth-century enthusiast's invention. Same number, one half survives checking. The test the book must also pass: which part of this was measured, and which part merely repeated? Pt.4 Ch.6

**Formal grammar as the answer to "that's just a metaphor"** — Plant growth is modelled in practice by a small symbolic rewriting system, and spots and stripes by two substances spreading and reacting at different rates with no template anywhere. The structure is produced each time by a rule short enough to write on a card, so the fern is an instance rather than a comparison. Pt.4 Ch.1

**The three returns — Brush, Tree, Forest** — A third axis, alongside Scale (who is involved) and Speed (how fast it turns). Those two describe conditions; this one describes what has been built and is still standing. Brush returns within the day and holds the ground; Tree takes years and is what you were growing; Forest stands after many lives and contains what others planted. The interaction rule generates the rest: **a disturbance moves what shares its timescale** — a bad day cannot have damaged your tree, a bad season genuinely bends one, a bad era reshapes the forest. The waste is all in the mismatches. Weather/Climate/Terrain reads a situation handed to you; Brush/Tree/Forest tends work you are growing. Pt.2 Ch.2, 11

**Vision — the Sacrifice Trap's Tending, read the other way** — Read only as a snare, the Sacrifice Trap is half a figure: a direction that makes today instrumental. A vision is not a destination, since force applies only to acceleration — it is a direction you check your acceleration against, and therefore a *hypothesis about what this Local Rule, repeated, would produce*. Falsifiable by design. The two readings separate on whether today survives the vision being wrong. Pt.2 Ch.11

**The rate pole** — Planting faster than you can tend. Commitments multiply rather than add (Pt.1 Ch.8's rule arithmetic), so the wall arrives suddenly; and the response to overload is *more* commitment, because compensatory control reaches for the feeling of agency and a fresh start supplies it while grinding through a half-built thing does not. Tell: excitement tracks recency of commitment. Where it reads as a snare, the lever is rule-level (a standing limit), never a schedule or a buffer. Deliberate overproduction is the same Tending read as a lifeline — one sign is whether an exit was built in. Pt.2 Ch.11

**Latency, and the felt difference between doing and undergoing** — A return that closes fast and clean registers as *something you did*; one that is slow or scrambled registers as *something that happened*, even when you are the one feeding it. Distance governs three things at once — fidelity, aimability, and how much is yours to answer for — which makes the Fold's cautions distance-dependent rather than flat: at one edge the opposite advice applies. Shortening the loop is an Information-flow move. Pt.2 Ch.12

**Who is feeding, who is absorbing** — Complementarity (Pt.2 Ch.14) already says the control axis produces fitted *opposite* rules rather than matching ones, which means a Dyad-Kin's two halves are often not comparable. So naming a Third has a second question: who supplies the rule and who receives the outcome. A loop can be reciprocal and not equitable, and if you are absorbing without feeding it is not yours to change. Pt.2 Ch.14, 16

**Agency is not responsibility** — That you can act on something does not make the not-acting a choice. Behavioral activation's own logic supplies the proof: the technique exists because waiting to feel like it does not work, so absent motivation is not evidence of unwillingness. And values persist beneath long inaction — a blocked route is a different object from a lost want. Pt.2 Ch.7

**Belonging as a need, not a preference** — Isolation produces craving in the same machinery as hunger, and connection is monitored continuously rather than after other needs are met. So removing a group's ideas removes nothing that was holding it: **you replace the function, not refute the content.** And belonging is made of repetition rather than agreement, which is why a club works where an argument does not. Second form of the Skein test, pointed at the person: *would I still believe this if leaving cost me nothing?* Pt.2 Ch.19

**Measured, modelled, offered** — The sixth instrument. A claim is checked and repeatable; or a structure that organizes what is known and earns its place by use; or a story put forward because it helps. All three legitimate, only the first is evidence, and the failure is a model or a story borrowing a measurement's authority — which happens to the author first. Pt.4 Ch.8

**The self extends to where your actions close a loop** — The active counterpart to the rubber hand: behind a wheel the boundary moves to the edge of the car, and nobody rear-ended says *he hit my car*. Not sensory agreement but a loop closing — action, consequence, fast enough to be felt as one event. The least mystical account of why what you repeatedly do stops feeling like something you do. Pt.4 Ch.2

**Fractals were invisible in plain sight** — Plants are full of them and are not what led anyone to the idea; the mathematics came from coastlines and curves treated as pathological. A pattern can be universal, unhidden, and genuinely unseeable until something gives you a way to hold it — which is the strongest available argument that the nine words do work rather than decoration. Pt.3 Ch.2

**Compost is a stock** — Not consolation for a particular loss but an accumulating reservoir that raises the growth rate of everything planted after. Why a fifth attempt is easier than a first, and not because the person improved. Pt.2 Ch.5

**Sensitive dependence, and the two unpredictabilities** — Two different things stop a Global Form being predictable, and they ask for different responses, so the book keeps them apart. *Sensitive dependence*: the same deterministic rule from two starting states differing by less than anyone can measure, diverging at a rate that compounds — which is why forecasting has a horizon rather than an accuracy problem, and why measuring harder does not help. *Unenumerable rules*: derived from the Nest rather than imported, since a Global Form at one scale is a Local Rule at the next, so no boundary drawn around a system is real and what crosses it was never interference. Pt.4 Ch.7 · Pt.1 Ch.8

**Fractal dimension** — A coastline has no length: shorten the ruler and the measurement grows without converging, because structure is present at every resolution. The dimension is the non-integer number saying how thoroughly a pattern fills the space it sits in — a line is 1, a plane is 2, Britain's west coast about 1.25. Turned on a situation it gives the exact form of why enumeration cannot finish: looking closer reveals more rule, not the last few rules. Pt.4 Ch.7

**Mechanism, not determinism** — The framework's actual commitment: causes exist and are findable. Compatible with determinism and with its denial, and the practice is identical under both, since either way your Local Rule is an input. The metaphysics does not reach the practice, which is why the book takes no position on it. Pt.4 Ch.7

**Fatalism as unavailable** — Not refuted, which would be weaker. "It was always going to happen" is a reading of the whole from outside the whole, and the Infinite Boundary already establishes there is no such position — so the claim can never be checked and never touches anything you might do differently. That shape is a paradigm, and the eighth rung says what to do with one. Pt.5 Ch.6 · Pt.4 Ch.7

**The strange loop** — A system containing a description of itself cannot complete that description from inside: the description must include itself describing, and it does not close. Used to say why the outside vantage is genuinely absent rather than merely hard to reach, and held as a regress rather than a proof — nothing is demonstrated, only followed until it stops terminating. Pt.4 Ch.7

**Climate, not trajectory** — What a Standing Third predicts, and the resolution of the predictive admission criterion against the limits of forecasting. Climate, not Tuesday: the shape a situation keeps returning to, never what happens next. Pt.5 Ch.7 · Pt.4 Ch.7

**The return path** — What separates Second-Order Design's poles, and the reason the figure has an intervention point at all. A rule-set always produces behavior its author never specified; that is the definition, not the failure. The Fraying is when what it produced reaches whoever could change the rule less and less, until it no longer does. Emergence you like can be Fraying; emergence you hate can be Tending. You never push on the emergence — you push below it, on Information flow. Pt.5 Ch.3, and its one-tier-up form in Proxy Detachment (Pt.5 Ch.4), where the measure *is* the channel and gaming it severs the channel while leaving it apparently intact.

**The parsimony rule** — The simplest reading that accounts for the behavior is the correct one, and you stop at it. Derived from elegance rather than added as a caution: a second figure that leaves your next action unchanged is a patch, and patches are counted here as everywhere else. Guards both failures — over-reading, which stacks figures until a person is a diagram and feels like insight the whole way down; and under-reading, which collapses back into what someone simply is. Pt.5 Ch.7

**The power gap** — Leverage can be real and not yours to reach. The ladder ranks rungs by how much they move and says nothing about which ones your position lets you touch, which is the most common route by which this book becomes self-blame for a reader doing everything right. Three options and only three: get access to a higher rung, leave where leaving is genuinely available, or stop paying for the unreachable rung and spend what you have where it reaches — the last being arithmetic rather than attitude, since effort aimed at an unreachable rung is withdrawn from a reachable one. Pt.2 Ch.10

**Shared-edge leverage** — A high-leverage connection is a maximally shared one, load-bearing for many downstream structures at once; a low-leverage one is peripheral, touching nothing beyond its own corner. Gives the leverage ladder an exact structural form rather than only a depth metaphor. Pt.4 Ch.3

**The intelligent-civilizations chain (the Drake equation) as a worked recursion chain** — Each term is another Local-Rule-to-Global-Form threshold (chemistry to life to complexity to intelligence to detectable technology); small real probabilities compounding multiplicatively across a chain, made vivid at the largest scale available. Pt.4 Ch.3

**Local Rule across scales, and resonance** — A single Local Rule projected forward, once per scale, since the same rule produces genuinely different Global Forms depending which scale you check it at, not simply "more of the same." Pt.2 Ch.3. Resonance — finding your exact situation already has a name — installs faster than derivation, which is the actual argument for why the Standing Thirds are characters rather than a plain list of tendencies. Pt.4 Ch.6

**The association machine, and the capacity to steer** — A mind doesn't observe and separately interpret; it fuses the two, which is why a harsh self-judgment feels like fact rather than guess. Naming a self-story is the manual, effortful version of pulling that fusion back apart by hand. Pt.2 Ch.4. Separately: a fast, reliable association is not automatically a beneficial one, and telling the two apart — then choosing to override a working pattern rather than simply ride it — is a real, non-default capacity, not a guarantee. This is named as the actual reason a practice is worth having at all, and returns at the close of the Woven Self in Part One and the close of Part Two (Closing the Loop). Pt.1 Ch.7 · Pt.2 Ch.20 · Pt.5 Ch.1 (The Nearest Light)
