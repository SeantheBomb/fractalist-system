The Loop Tongue is a language, and languages are learned by use. Three exercises, in increasing difficulty.

**Solo: Translate one real loop.** Pick a genuine pattern in your own life — something from your Day 1 or Day 4 onboarding notice. Write it first as an ordinary sentence. Then write it again using the notation from Chapter 3, arrows and loop-marks and all. Then say, out loud to no one, what scale you were speaking at. This exercise alone tends to surface leverage points a purely verbal description misses — seeing the arrows on paper makes it obvious, sometimes, that you've been trying to change a step near the end of the loop when the actual leverage sits near the beginning.

**Paired: The mirror exercise.** With another person — ideally someone also working through this book — one of you describes a real, live tension in your life in ordinary linear language, uninterrupted, for two minutes. The other person's only job afterward is to reflect it back as a Loop: "So it sounds like — [Local Rule] → [Local Rule] → back to the start ↻. Is that right?" The speaker corrects the sketch until it actually matches. This is harder than it sounds and reveals, quickly, how much linear storytelling smuggles in a villain by default.

**Group: A live Spiral Check** on any real, current decision your group is actually facing — not a hypothetical. Use the ritual from Chapter 6 exactly as written, including the rule against repeating the previous person's scale. Debrief afterward: did the decision change, or did it just get better-supported? Both outcomes count as the exercise working.
