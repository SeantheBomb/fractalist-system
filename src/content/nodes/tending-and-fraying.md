Every Third carries two readings rather than one — not opposite meanings assigned arbitrarily, but the same figure running in one of the only two directions a running loop can move. **Tending** is the Third's loop strengthening, each turn feeding the next a little more. **Fraying** is the same loop weakening, each turn feeding the next a little less. A rope pulled tight can be a lifeline or a snare; which one depends on what it is tied to.

There is no reversed orientation to fuss over, no upside-down card to interpret by convention — a <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/weaving/">Weaving</a> determines Tending or Fraying by context, not by chance. When a Third is drawn, ask plainly: is this pattern, in the situation actually in front of you, running stronger or running weaker? Sometimes this is obvious the moment the card is named. Sometimes it's the entire question the Weaving was performed to answer, and sitting with the ambiguity honestly is more useful than forcing a quick verdict either way.

This is worth being direct about, because it's the single most important honesty check in this whole practice: **Tending is not "good news" and Fraying is not "bad news."** A Weaving is not laid out to find out whether things will go well. It's laid out to see a shape more clearly than one flat angle allows. <a class="concept-link" href="/thirds/held-silence/">Held Silence</a> read as Tending is not a blessing, and read as Fraying is not a curse. Either is an accurate description of a pattern already present, named so it can be seen rather than left to keep operating in the dark.
