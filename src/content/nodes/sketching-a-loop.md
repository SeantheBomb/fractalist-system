Most people never draw these. If you're not someone who reaches for a pen mid-conversation, skip to the next chapter — nothing later in this book depends on it.

For everyone else: words alone still carry some of the linear bias baked into speech, and four marks remove more of it. Learnable in under a minute, sketchable on a napkin.

**An arrow (→)** means "feeds into." *Anxiety → checking the phone.*

**A closed loop (↻)** placed after a chain means the chain returns to its own start — a reinforcing <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/loop/">Loop</a>, where each lap makes the next lap more likely. *Anxiety → checking the phone → brief relief → faster anxiety next time ↻*

**A half-loop (⤸)** means a balancing Loop — one that dampens itself rather than reinforcing, returning toward a middle instead of accelerating away from it. *Overwork → exhaustion → forced rest ⤸*

**A nested bracket [ ]** around a chain means "this whole pattern is itself one <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/local-rule/">Local Rule</a> inside a larger version of the same Loop, one scale up." *[Anxiety → checking the phone ↻] → a culture of distraction, built the same way, one person at a time.*

**One more thing worth knowing, because it tells you which mark to actually draw rather than leaving it to guesswork: every arrow in a chain carries a sign, and the loop's own type follows from those signs exactly, not by feel.** An arrow is "+" if more of the cause produces more of the effect, "−" if more of the cause produces less of it. Trace the whole loop, note the sign of every link, and count how many are negative. An even count — including zero — makes it reinforcing. An odd count makes it balancing. *Anxiety → checking the phone* is a "+" link; *checking the phone → relief* is a "+" link; *relief → faster anxiety next time* is a "+" link — zero negatives, even, reinforcing, exactly matching the ↻ above. This isn't a metaphor for how loops work. It's the actual arithmetic underneath the mark, and it means you never have to guess which symbol to draw — you can derive it, every time, from the chain itself.

That's the entire notation — not a replacement for a formal systems diagram, just the version two people can use standing in a kitchen, mid-argument, when one of them says "wait, draw what you mean."
