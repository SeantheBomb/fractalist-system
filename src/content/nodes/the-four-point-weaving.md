The standard form, used for an actual conundrum rather than daily reflection.

Before laying the cards out, it's worth saying once, out loud or to yourself, what <a class="concept-link" href="/thirds/infinite-boundary/">the Infinite Boundary</a> already told you without needing a card: whatever these four show, the whole reading is still one small, summed part of something no single <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/weaving/">Weaving</a> was ever going to capture completely. This isn't a disclaimer added out of modesty. It's the actual shape of the thing — four fixed views, real and worth having, inside a boundary no view from within it could ever draw.

Draw one card for each ring — Self-Kin, Dyad-Kin, Skein-Kin, and Culture-Kin. Lay them in the nested arrangement from Chapter 3, Self innermost, Culture outermost. Read from the inside ring outward, or from the outside in — the Self ring for what's happening in you, the Dyad ring for what's happening between you and one other person, the Skein ring for what's happening at the scale of the group around you, the Culture ring for what's happening at a scale beyond any single group you could name. The four rings are the whole reading, and none of them gets special priority just for being drawn first off the deck.

Do not treat the four rings as needing to agree with each other. A Self-ring card in Tending sitting beside a Skein-ring card in Fraying is not a contradiction to resolve — it's exactly the kind of information a single flat view would have missed entirely: one pattern may be running stronger at your own scale while another is genuinely fraying at the scale of the group around you, and knowing that is the entire value of having drawn four views instead of one.
