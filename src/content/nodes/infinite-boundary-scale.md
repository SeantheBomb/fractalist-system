**<a class="concept-link" href="/thirds/infinite-boundary/">The Infinite Boundary</a>** is every figure above, aggregated and never averaged — the only entry in this catalogue with no Tending and no Fraying pole. It follows directly from the rules already in play: a pole is read from a vantage, every vantage sits at some scale, and the recursion chain terminates here. There is no scale above this one, therefore no vantage, therefore no pole. Mathematics has a name for an object every other object maps into, in exactly one way — a terminal object, and the word is borrowed for its shape rather than as a proof. What terminates has no outside.

Practitioners have taken to calling it, with equal parts irreverence and precision, the Greatest Objective Denominator — not without noticing what the initials spell, and not entirely joking either. The mathematics of the word matters: a common denominator is the single base a set of fractions is expressed over, however different their numerators. Not the one trait every pattern shares, which would be a compression — the one thing every pattern is ultimately expressed in terms of, which is the whole.

**And the honest thing to say about the joke, since a reader who holds a faith will already have noticed it.** Everything running everywhere, all at once, interacting — that aggregate does produce something. Not a will in any sense this book can defend, because a will requires a vantage and there is no outside from which to hold one. But the sum of every pattern in motion does have a *drift* at any given moment, and that drift is not authored by anyone, is not reachable by petition, and is nonetheless the largest real thing there is.

If you already believe in something above this, nothing in Fractalism contradicts it, and this is the seam where the two meet. Reading the emergent behavior of every system interacting at once is the closest thing to reading a god that this framework will ever offer — and it is offered as the nearest available honest description, not as a replacement for a faith, not as an argument against one, and not as a smaller god wearing different clothes. What a practitioner does with that is theirs. The framework stops precisely here, and it stops on purpose.

**One consequence of all this, which is the most practically useful thing in the chapter and is easy to walk past.** If there is no vantage outside the totality, then nobody occupies the seat from which the whole could be read as finished. Which means *"it was always going to happen this way"* is not a hard truth you are required to make peace with. It is a sentence that could only be checked from a position nobody has ever held. Fatalism does not get refuted here — it gets something worse done to it: it becomes unavailable, permanently, a claim that can never be checked and never touch anything you might do differently. Treat it the way Part Two's leverage ladder says to treat any paradigm: as a thing you can pick up and look at, not a floor to stand on. Part Four's chapter on running it forward does this argument properly.

It has no will. It is not addressing you. It is where the recursion chain terminates, and nothing more.
