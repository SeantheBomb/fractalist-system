Most systems that lay symbolic cards out in a sequence read them left to right, as a story — past, present, future; cause, then effect. This book has spent its entire length arguing against exactly that shape for anything that actually matters, and a <a class="concept-link" href="/concept/weaving/">Weaving</a> should not quietly reintroduce it through the back door.

A Weaving is laid out as **nested rings**, not a row. The Self position sits innermost, drawn from the twelve Currents. The Dyad position sits in the ring around it, drawn from the Meetings. The Skein position sits around that, drawn from the Weaves. The Culture position sits outermost of the four you actually draw, from the Long Forms. <a class="concept-link" href="/thirds/infinite-boundary/">The Infinite Boundary</a> is never placed as a card at all — it's simply understood to hold the whole arrangement, the same way it holds everything else, and needn't be said aloud more than once.

This is not decoration. It's the direct, physical enactment of the Nest vocabulary from the Loop Tongue: a Self is nested inside a Dyad's reach, which is nested inside a Skein's reach, which is nested inside whatever Culture-scale pattern it feeds — exactly the way the rings sit inside each other on the table or the cloth, and exactly the recursion chain Part One already named. And it enacts the orthographic-to-perspective claim from Chapter 0 just as directly — you read a Weaving from the inside ring outward, or the outside in, but never as a timeline, because nothing about it is a timeline. It's one situation, viewed at four nested resolutions simultaneously.

If you're weaving without physical cards, hold this in your head instead of on a table: four concentric answers to the same question, not four chapters of one story.
